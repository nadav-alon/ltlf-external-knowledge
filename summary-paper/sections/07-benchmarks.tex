\section{Benchmarks}\label{sec:benchmarks}

\subsection{Research Questions}
\guide{What the experiments answer. E.g.\ RQ1: do the methods agree and verify? RQ2: how much does
knowledge shrink the product? RQ3: speed vs.\ \tool{ltlfsynt}? RQ4: which method wins where?}
We aim to answer the following research questions in our experiments:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[RQ1] Verify equivalence of methods in terms of realizability and correctness of the synthesized controller.
  \item[RQ2] Quantify the impact of external knowledge on solve time, compared to itself without knowledge and a baseline tool.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Metrics}
\guide{Structural (state counts, deterministic, committed) vs.\ timing (wall clock, median of
repeats). Prune ratio; nk-product ratio; speedup. Why state counts are the decisive number.}

We compare the following metrics in our experiments:
\begin{itemize}
  \item State counts of the automata
  \item Wall clock time for synthesis
  \item Amount of pruning achieved by the knowledge transducers, as compared to the unpruned product
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baselines}
\guide{\tool{ltlfsynt} on the $\psiin\rightarrow\varphi$ encoding; the no-knowledge product;
the unpruned product.}

The ltlfsynt tool from Spot is used as a baseline for comparison, as well as the no-knowledge product and the unpruned product.

\subsection{Benchmark Families}
\guide{One paragraph per family: $\varphi_n$, knowledge, what it isolates. Then the
summary table.}

We compared only input knowledge; as such, every family has trivial $\Tout$. Every family also comes in a realizable and an unrealizable variant.

\subsubsection{Parametric Families}
\guide{cons-prunes, cons-inert, mirror-small, mirror-degenerate, parity-t3, knowledge-chain(-inert),
co-generated, \ldots}

\textbf{\texttt{cons-prunes}} fixes the known input $k$ to true, so $\cons$ cuts an exponential goal to $n+1$ states; \textbf{\texttt{cons-inert}} makes $k$ a free output, the no-pruning control.
\textbf{\texttt{mirror-small}} and \textbf{\texttt{mirror-degenerate}} have no knowledge and separate the NFA and MTDFA routes, in favour of the NFA and against it.
\textbf{\texttt{knowledge-chain}} and \textbf{\texttt{knowledge-chain-inert}} grow $|\Tin|$ to $n$, with and without pruning; the inert one hits $|\text{product}| = |\Tin| \cdot |\text{goal}|$.
\textbf{\texttt{parity-t3}} uses a parity $\Tin$, which no $\LTLf$ formula expresses.
\textbf{\texttt{co-generated}} uses a $2^n$-state delay line $k_t = a_{t-n}$ that $\varphi_n$ needs to be realizable.

\subsubsection{Engineered Domain Families}
\guide{Domain-inspired instances (e.g.\ slippery world, hidden counters).}

\textbf{\texttt{slippery-*}}: reach a corner of a $2^n \times 2^n$ grid where a free input $\mathit{slip}$ doubles a move; $\Tin$ tracks the known position, and the three variants differ only in how $\psiin$ encodes it.
\textbf{\texttt{hidden-counter}}: $\Tin$ counts $a$'s modulo $2^n$ and reveals the top bit as $k$, which the output must predict one step ahead (realizable) or two (unrealizable).

\subsubsection{External Suites}
SYNTCOMP \texttt{tlsf-fin}: $1717$ instances with no knowledge of their own.
Dependency extraction (\cref{sec:dependencies}) finds knowledge in $6$ of the $388$ that could be analysed among the $430$ instances with at most $16$ propositions, all unrealizable; a seventh, with $26$ propositions, lies above that bound.

\subsubsection{Generated Corpus}
\guide{Random instances used for oracle soak testing.}

Fixed-seed random $(\varphi, \text{partition}, \Tin)$ triples, untimed, checked for agreement with \tool{ltlfsynt} and by model-checking each controller (RQ1).

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
The timing sweep ran on the HPC cluster (nodes \texttt{cn01} and \texttt{cn16}, Intel Xeon Gold 6130 at 2.10\,GHz) from a Release build at commit \texttt{4e3beec} with Spot 2.15.1.
Each cell is repeated 5 times with a 20\,s timeout, for $n$ up to 6; the committed run is \texttt{docs/runs/2026-11-14-summary-paper-sweep.json}.
The RQ1 correctness checks ran on the developer's desktop, not on the HPC, and are machine-independent.
The SYNTCOMP verdict cross-checks come from the 2026-08-18 SYNTCOMP-fin sweep (\texttt{docs/runs/2026-08-18-syntcomp-fin-sweep.json}), run on the desktop with tools built at commit \texttt{40c5d72} and Spot 2.15.1.
The generated-corpus checks are oracle soaks with no committed run.
Repeats are aggregated by their median.
