\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\guide{One paragraph: problem, approach, main result, next step.}

We studied $\LTLf$ synthesis in which external transducers $\Tin$ and $\Tout$ fix some inputs and outputs, and solved it with NFA, DFA and on-the-fly products of the goal automaton with the transducers, which agree on every benchmark and with \tool{ltlfsynt}.
Input knowledge pays off when folding it into the formula is expensive, at least $9331\times$ on \texttt{co-generated}, where the run without knowledge times out, but that is a purpose-built example, the other one, \texttt{slippery-*}, gains nothing measurable ($0.55$--$1.13\times$), and we could not find such cases in existing benchmarks.
With output dependencies on the TACAS'24 families, \tool{ltlfsynt} is faster than our method wherever it finishes.
Next, we will port the LTL benchmarks of the TACAS'24 paper to $\LTLf$ for a like-for-like comparison.
