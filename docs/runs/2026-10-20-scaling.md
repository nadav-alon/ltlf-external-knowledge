## 1. Headline

EK beats both the folded formula and ltlfsynt on the hidden counter and on slippery's one-hot arm, and its lead grows with N on both. It still loses on slippery's binary-compact arm, which states the same transducer as a short formula.

| family | EK vs formula (same solver, both simplified) | EK vs ltlfsynt internal |
|---|---|---|
| hidden-counter | 1.02x (N=1) → 1.64x (N=8) → 2.18x (N=10) → 1.52x (N=11) → 2.23x (N=12) → **3.83x (N=13)** | 0.95x → 1.70x → 2.24x → 1.50x → 1.98x → **3.09x (N=13)** |
| slippery, one-hot | 1.75x (N=6, #178) → 3.17x (N=7) → **3.69x (N=8)** | 2.39x (N=6, #178) → 4.17x (N=7) → **4.10x (N=8, one cell; ≥ 3.41x with ltlfsynt's realizable timeout)** |
| slippery, binary-compact (control) | 0.73x (N=7) → 0.92x (N=8), formula faster | 0.40x (N=7) → 0.40x (N=8), ltlfsynt faster |

- **What separates the arms is the size of ψ_in, not of T_in.** At N=8 both slippery arms have |T_in| = 65 536 states. The one-hot ψ_in has 3 701 247 formula nodes (56x |T_in|); the binary-compact ψ_in has 1 651. EK wins where folding the knowledge in produces a large formula, and loses where the same knowledge folds in small.
- **The hidden counter is not exponentially hard to state.** |ψ_in| / |T_in| settles at 15.5 from N=6 on (section 3): our LTLf ψ_in grows linearly with the transducer, so this family does not show the succinctness gap #146 looked for. EK's lead here comes from solving, not from a smaller input.
- **EK explores more, not fewer, roots on the hidden counter** (explored-roots ratio rising to 2.0) and is still faster: its roots are cheaper to expand than the folded formula's.
- The minimized product is smaller with the knowledge as a transducer at every N measured (minimized nk 0.58–0.97, N=1..10), but the gap shrinks with N (0.97 at N=10).
- Two cells per N (one realizable, one unrealizable), repeat 3: a trend, not a distribution. The N=11 dip comes with the move from the desktop to the cluster.
