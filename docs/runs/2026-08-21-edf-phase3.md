## 1. The blocker — D5's per-bit weak `X` does not survive the trace boundary

**Witness** (extracted by `/developer` with a throwaway diagnostic, then
discarded): at $n = 2$, both goals, a **length-1** trace —

```
{ !slip, pos = (0,0), no mv }
```

$\Tin$ produces it; the compact $A_N$ rejects it. Same witness at $n = 3$.

**Why**, and this is the part worth reading:

- Under **weak `X`**, at a trace's last position `X f` is vacuously **true** for
  any `f`. D6 leans on exactly this to make $A_N$ agree with "$\delta$ undefined
  past the end".
- That works when one `X` wraps a whole destination cube — which is what the
  **enumerated** arms do (`X(<the entire destination conjunction>)`). Vacuously
  true, rule satisfied, boundary handled.
- D5's compact predicates instead write **per-bit** $\mathsf{X} b_i
  \leftrightarrow \mathrm{rhs}_i$. At the boundary $\mathsf{X} b_i$ is still
  vacuously true, but $(\mathsf{X} b_i \leftrightarrow \mathrm{rhs}_i)$ collapses
  to $\mathrm{rhs}_i$ — **not** to true.
- For $\mathrm{Keep}$ ($\mathrm{rhs}_i = b_i$) the rule therefore demands **every
  bit set** at the final position. At the start cell (all bits $0$) it is
  violated, so any trace ending on a non-all-ones cell under a Keep-class move is
  spuriously rejected. The length-1 stutter at $(0,0)$ is the shortest instance.

So the compact $A_N$ as written is **too strong**, and the direction is
consistent with everything measured: the certificate is red on all six sampled
cases ($n = 2, 3, 4$ × both goals), and `BenchSuiteCrossMethodAgreement`
independently shows the `-nk` subjects (which route through `psi_in`) dissenting
from the raw-$\Tin$ methods on the centre goal at $n = 2, 3$.

**This is a property of D5's math read literally, not a translation typo.** The
`/developer` agent implemented D5 exactly as pinned — which is what it was told
to do, and why the failure is informative rather than noise. `\lnot` does not
distribute through weak `X` at a boundary, and D5's shape assumes it does.

**Candidate fixes exist, and picking one is the decision this run stops on** —
see §5.1. They are not cosmetically equivalent: they differ in whether D8's
conjunct-count claim survives, and whether D6's "weak `X` is mandatory" rationale
still reads correctly for this arm.

## 4. A second, independent finding: D8's "15 conjuncts" is not what Spot counts

`SlipperyBinaryCompactStructural.ANHasExactlyFifteenTopLevelConjunctsIndependentOfN`
fails with measured top-level conjunct counts:

| $n$ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `an.size()` | 18 | 20 | 22 | 24 | 26 |

That is exactly $\mathbf{14 + 2n}$, and the cause is arithmetic, not a bug: D5's
init conjunct $\mathrm{Min}(b^x) \wedge \mathrm{Min}(b^y)$ is **$2n$ literals**,
which Spot flattens into the top-level `AND` rather than keeping as "1 init
conjunct". $14 + 2n$: fourteen rules plus $2n$ init literals.

**The separation argument survives this; the stated number does not.** $14 + 2n$
is still $O(\log N)$ against the enumerated arms' $14N + 1$, so the "constant vs
linear in $N$" contrast that D8 and Stop-list 2 rest on is intact in substance.
But D8 asserts $15$ as **"exact and derived from the construction"**, and it is
not, so the assertion as written is wrong and the PRD text needs a decision:
count the init block as one grouped conjunct, or correct the claim to $14 + 2n$.

**Consequence — the separation is currently UNMEASURED, not disproven.** T9
(`DfaOfANIsAtLeastFourToTheN...`) fails at line 252 on its own *sanity
precondition*, which re-asserts the same $15$, and so **never evaluates**
$\lvert\mathrm{DFA}(A_N)\rvert \ge 4^n$. No DFA size for the compact arm was
measured this run, and deliberately so: $A_N$ is currently the wrong formula
(§1), so measuring its DFA would produce a number about a construction that is
going to change. The headline claim of the whole PRD remains open.

## 5. Questions for the evening grill

### 5.1 The blocker: how should D5 handle the trace boundary?

The one decision that unblocks Phase 3. Three shapes, and they are **not**
equivalent in what they cost:

1. **Wrap each axis's whole update body under one outer `X(...)`**, mirroring
   what the enumerated arms do. Closest to the working precedent. But the
   right-hand sides refer to the *current* bit values while the left refer to the
   next, so this is not a mechanical rewrap — the current-state values have to be
   folded into the guard, and folding them in *by enumeration* is exactly the
   compactness the arm exists to avoid. Needs a real formulation, not a bracket
   move.
2. **An explicit boundary escape hatch** — make each rule vacuous at the last
   position, e.g. guard the update with a "there is a next position" condition so
   the implication is trivially satisfied at the end. Keeps D5's per-bit ripple
   shape verbatim. Costs one extra conjunct-or-literal per rule, so D8's count
   changes again (and §4 has to be settled anyway).
3. **Something else you have in mind** — this is bespoke math you wrote, and I am
   not going to invent a fourth option and call it yours.

My reading: **(2)** looks like the smaller blast radius — it preserves the
ripple-carry construction the arm is *about*, and the conjunct count is being
restated anyway per §4 — but (1) is the one with a proven-working precedent in
this very PRD, and which of those two matters more is a judgement about what the
compact arm is meant to demonstrate. That is yours.
