## 3. Findings

### F1 — `NfaProduct` is the one-hot arm's wall, and it arrives at $n = 3$

Measured across all twenty $(\text{method}, \text{arm}, n)$ cells at $n = 2, 3$:
nineteen finish in **under 1 s**. The twentieth, `nfa-product` on
`slippery-onehot` at $n = 3$, takes

| goal | `product_construction` | of which `determinize` | wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| corner | 407.0 s | 199.3 s | 407.0 s |
| centre | 432.8 s | 200.9 s | 432.8 s |

— ~7 min per goal, and the same in the `-nk` column. It **does** terminate; it is
slow, not stuck.

This is PRD Stop-list 8 (*"one-hot dying at large $n$ is a reportable data point,
not a bug to tune around"*) arriving at $n = 3$ rather than $n = 6$. Two things
make it a clean finding rather than a defect:

- it is **NfaProduct-specific, not MONA-specific** — `MtnfaProduct` also goes
  through MONA and is instant on the same cell;
- it is **encoding-driven** — one-hot at $n = 3$ carries $2\cdot 2^3 = 16$
  position APs against binary's 6, and the cost sits in determinizing the
  NFA product, not in the automaton construction (0.0 s).

**Handling:** the cell is excluded *by construction* from the two cross-method
tests, with the measurement written at the exclusion site, rather than by a
wall-clock race (`run_bench_case` has no cancellation hook, so a race would have
to detach a thread — see F2 — which a test binary must not do). The other four
methods still cross-check each other on that cell, so T2 keeps its teeth.

### F2 — `ltlf-ek-bench` **SIGSEGVs on any per-case timeout and loses the whole report**

Pre-existing, latent since Phase 2, and newly reachable now that a family can
exceed the budget. `RunCaseWithTimeout` (`src/ltlf_ek_bench.cpp:283`) bounds a
case by running it on a thread and, on a deadline miss, **detaching** it. The
detached worker is still inside Spot/MONA when the process exits, so the run ends
in a segfault during static destruction — **before `--out` is written**.

Reproduced deterministically: `--families=slippery-onehot --subjects=nfa-product
--n-min=3 --n-max=3 --timeout=10` exits **139** after ~11 s and writes no JSON at
all. The same command with `--timeout=3000` exits 0 and writes a complete report.

This matters beyond this phase: **PRD Stop-list 8 instructs a timing-out row to
be recorded as `TIMEOUT` and the other arms continued, and the runner cannot do
that** — one timeout destroys the entire sweep. Phase 4 sweeps $n = 2\ldots6$
with a 60 s per-case budget and *expects* one-hot to time out, so Phase 4 is
blocked on this.

**Not fixed here, deliberately.** The runner belongs to
`docs/prd/benchmark-suite.md`, not to this PRD, and the fix is a design choice
(a forked child process per case is the only way to actually cancel a blocking
Spot call) — that is a change to a settled part of the harness and a user's call,
not a day-run's. Recorded in `docs/BACKLOG.md`.

### F3 — "$14N+1$ top-level conjuncts" needs a flatten-aware reading

The generator emits exactly what D3 specifies: 1 initial-cell conjunct + $2\times
7N$ rules. But `spot::op::And` is **n-ary and flattens**, so the initial-cell
conjunct's own literals (a conjunction of $2n$ binary / $2N$ one-hot literals)
become children of the same top-level `And`, and `formula::size()` reads
$14N + 2n$ (resp. $14N + 2N$) — 60 rather than 57 at binary $N = 4$, 64 rather
than 57 at one-hot $N = 4$.

The claim is true; the naive counting was not. T5 now asserts it the way it is
*constructed* — exactly $14N$ `G`-rooted rules, plus a remainder that must be
**literals only** — which is also the form that stays comparable to Phase 3's
compact arm (14 `G`-rules against $14N$). No formula was changed to make a
number come out.
