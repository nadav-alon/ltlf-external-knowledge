\begin{frame}{What is this}

  A \alert{prototype} that runs five synthesis methods under one interface, and a prototype benchmark suite.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item Five methods that produce controllers from a goal formula, a variable partition, and a knowledge transducer.
    \item A parametric benchmark suite, raced against \texttt{ltlfsynt} where the comparison is legitimate.
  \end{itemize}

\end{frame}

% =========================== ACT 1 -- THE TOOLS ============================
\section{The tools}

\begin{frame}{The Methods}

  Every method takes a goal formula $\varphi$, a variable partition $(\Ifree, \Iknown, \Ofree, \Oknown)$, and two knowledge transducers $\Tin$, $\Tout$.

  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Method & Representation & Built on \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{DfaProduct}       & explicit DFA      & Spot \\
    \texttt{NfaProduct}       & explicit NFA      & \textsc{mona} \\
    \texttt{MtdfaProduct}     & symbolic (MTDFA)  & Spot \\
    \texttt{MtnfaProduct}     & symbolic (MTNFA)  & \textsc{mona} \\
    \texttt{OtfMtdfaProduct}  & symbolic, on-the-fly & Spot \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Two binaries}

  \textbf{\texttt{ltlf-ek-synth}} - the synthesizer
  \begin{shelltiny}
--dfa-product | --nfa-product | --mtdfa-product | --mtnfa-product | --otf-mtdfa-product
--formula=STRING          the goal formula
--part-file PATH          the variable partition   (or --inputs/--outputs shorthand)
--known-input-transducer  T_in     --known-output-transducer  T_out
--realizable              verdict only        --model-check   verify a controller
--benchmark PATH          per-stage timing report as JSON
  \end{shelltiny}

  \vspace{2mm}
  \textbf{\texttt{ltlf-ek-deps}} - finds dependent variables. Very tentative.
  \begin{shelltiny}
--formula=STRING          --direction in|out
--transducer PATH         write the resulting T_in / T_out
--emit-part PATH          write the updated part file
  \end{shelltiny}
  {\scriptsize Finds a dependent input set $X_{dep}$ or dependent output set
  $Y_{dep}$, and can emit it as a ready-to-use transducer.}
\end{frame}

% =========================== ACT 2 -- USAGE ================================
\section{Using it}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{One instance, in full}

  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \textbf{The partition} --- a part file:
      \begin{shelltiny}
input_free:   a
input_known:  k
output_free:  o
output_known:
      \end{shelltiny}

      \vspace{1mm}
      \textbf{The goal:} $\varphi = \texttt{X[!] k}$ \\
      {\scriptsize ``at the next step, $k$ holds'' --- a property about a
      \emph{known} variable, whose value the controller does not choose.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
      \textbf{The knowledge} --- $\Tin$ as HOA plus a labelling:
      \begin{shelltiny}
States: 2   AP: 2 "a" "k"
--BODY--
State: 0
  [t] 1
State: 1
  [t] 1
--END--
%%LAMBDA
state 0: !k
state 1: k
      \end{shelltiny}
      {\scriptsize $k$ is false initially, true from step 1 onward ---
      regardless of what the environment plays.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The knowledge is load-bearing}

  Same $\varphi$, same partition, same method. \alert{Only $\Tin$ changes.}

  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
      {\scriptsize $\Tin$ = \texttt{tin-eventual-k}: $k$ turns on at step 1
      and stays on.}
      \begin{shelltiny}
$ ltlf-ek-synth --dfa-product \
    --formula="X[!] k" \
    --part-file part-a-k-o.txt \
    --known-input-transducer tin-eventual-k.hoa

HOA: v1
States: 2
...
exit code 0                (a controller)
      \end{shelltiny}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
      {\scriptsize $\Tin$ = \texttt{tin-delay-k}: $k$ tracks the environment's
      $a$, one step behind.}
      \begin{shelltiny}
$ ltlf-ek-synth --dfa-product \
    --formula="X[!] k" \
    --part-file part-a-k-o.txt \
    --known-input-transducer tin-delay-k.hoa

UNREALIZABLE
exit code 20
      \end{shelltiny}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \vfill
  {\small The verdict is a property of the \emph{knowledge}, not of the
  formula. Nothing the controller can play changes either outcome --- $k$ is
  not its variable.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The output is a checkable artifact}

  Realizable does not just print \texttt{REALIZABLE} --- it prints the
  controller, as a Spot automaton:

  \begin{shelltiny}
HOA: v1
States: 2
Start: 0
AP: 2 "a" "o"
acc-name: all
properties: trans-labels explicit-labels state-acc complete deterministic
spot-state-player: 0 1
controllable-AP: 1
--BODY--
State: 0    [t] 1
State: 1    [t] 0
--END--
  \end{shelltiny}

  \vspace{1mm}
  \dots{} and the same binary will check one for you:

  \begin{shelltiny}
$ ltlf-ek-synth --dfa-product --formula="X[!] k" --part-file part-a-k-o.txt \
    --known-input-transducer tin-eventual-k.hoa --model-check
SAFE
  \end{shelltiny}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Producing knowledge, not just consuming it}

  \texttt{ltlf-ek-deps} asks which variables are \emph{forced} by the history
  --- and can emit the answer as a transducer the methods then consume.

  \begin{shelltiny}
$ ltlf-ek-deps --formula="F(a ^ b)" --inputs a,b --outputs o --direction in
dependent inputs: a   (of a, b)

$ ltlf-ek-deps --formula="F(a ^ b)" --inputs a,b --outputs o --direction out
dependent outputs: none
  \end{shelltiny}

  \vspace{1mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$\Xdep$ non-empty:} the environment's $a$ is forced --- the
          symmetric question, run against $\neg\varphi$.
    \item \textbf{$\Ydep$ correctly empty:} the system can always violate an
          output conjunct unconditionally, so nothing on the output side is
          ever forced here. Structural, not a gap in the tool.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% =========================== ACT 3 -- MEASUREMENTS =========================
\section{Early results}

\begin{frame}{What was measured}

  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \textbf{Four parametric families}, each scaling in $n$:
      \begin{itemize}\small
        \item \texttt{cons-prunes} --- the consistency filter $\cons$
              genuinely cuts the product
        \item \texttt{cons-inert} --- $\cons$ cuts nothing
        \item \texttt{mirror-small}, \texttt{mirror-degenerate}
      \end{itemize}
      \vspace{1mm}
      {\scriptsize A fifth family, \texttt{parity-t3}, is excluded: it sits in
      an expressibility tier the comparison bars, and its declared
      realizability is wrong.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \textbf{Provenance}
      \begin{center}\scriptsize
      \begin{tabular}{ll}
        \toprule
        build      & \alert{Release} \\
        Spot       & 2.15.1 \\
        $n$        & 2 \dots 8, all pairs reached 8 \\
        repeats    & 3, 20\,s per-case timeout \\
        sweep      & no case stopped early \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}
      \end{center}
      \vspace{1mm}
      {\scriptsize All five methods run every case; ratios are taken against
      \texttt{MtdfaProduct}.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Speedup relative to \texttt{MtdfaProduct}}
  \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/speedup.pdf}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{-1mm}
  {\small \textbf{One clean signal:} \texttt{OtfMtdfaProduct} pulls away
  (\alert{4.4$\times$} at $n=8$) exactly on \texttt{cons-prunes} --- and costs
  essentially nothing (0.91--0.95$\times$) on the three families where it does
  not help.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why: the goal blows up, the product does not}
  \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/structural.pdf}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{-1mm}
  {\small Sizes, not times --- so this panel is build-independent. On
  \texttt{cons-prunes} the goal DFA grows exponentially while the product stays
  linear: that gap is the thing the on-the-fly method is exploiting. Where the
  two curves coincide, it has nothing to exploit.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Absolute cost, and what \texttt{ltlfsynt} costs}
  \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/cost.pdf}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{-1mm}
  {\small Two things that are \alert{not} algorithmic results. The
  \textsc{mona}-backed methods sit 1--2 orders higher throughout --- that is
  the shell-out. And \texttt{ltlfsynt}'s line is \alert{flat}: it grows
  $1.06$--$1.09\times$ from $n=2$ to $n=8$ while \texttt{MtdfaProduct} grows
  $5.7\times$ and the goal DFA grows $5 \to 257$ states.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why the \texttt{ltlfsynt} line is flat}

  Its whole-process time never leaves \textbf{4.98--5.74\,ms} --- across every
  family, every $n$, both polarities.

  \begin{itemize}
    \item That is a \alert{floor set by process startup}: \texttt{fork},
          \texttt{exec}, dynamic linking. Our methods are measured
          \emph{in-process}.
    \item So at these sizes the comparison \textbf{cannot discriminate} on
          time. ``$14\times$ faster at $n=8$'' would be a statement about
          subprocess spawn, not about synthesis.
    \item What the race \emph{does} establish is the verdicts --- next slide.
  \end{itemize}

  \vfill
  {\small The honest reading: to compare synthesis cost against
  \texttt{ltlfsynt} we need instances big enough to clear the $\sim$5\,ms
  floor. None of these four families get there by $n=8$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Agreement with \texttt{ltlfsynt}}

  \begin{center}
    {\Huge \textbf{\textcolor{slotone}{0}} verdict mismatches} \\[1mm]
    {\large over \textbf{56} comparable cases}
  \end{center}

  \vfill
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Every case in the four families is re-encoded and raced against
          \texttt{ltlfsynt}; both verdicts are recorded per case.
    \item 14 further cases are \emph{skipped by construction}, not silently
          passed --- their tier makes the monolithic encoding illegitimate.
  \end{itemize}

  \vfill
  {\small A cross-check on the verdicts, not on the timings --- and it does
  not settle the reduction itself (appendix).}
\end{frame}

% =========================== CLOSE =========================================
\begin{frame}{Where this leaves us}

  \textbf{The prototype:} five interchangeable methods, two tools, controllers
  that can be emitted and checked, and a benchmark suite that reruns itself.

  \vspace{2mm}
  \textbf{The early results:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item On-the-fly product is \alert{roughly free when the consistency
          filter is inert} and \alert{4.4$\times$ when it prunes} --- and the
          structural data says why.
    \item No verdict disagreement with \texttt{ltlfsynt} anywhere the
          comparison is legitimate.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{2mm}
  \textbf{What these numbers are not:} four synthetic families, $n \le 8$, one
  machine. A signal about where to look, not a cost model.

  \vspace{2mm}
  \textbf{Open:} the monolithic reduction (appendix), and the expressibility
  tier that currently bars a family from comparison.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Appendix --- the O5 boundary}

  On a \emph{partial} transducer our verdict and \texttt{ltlfsynt}'s verdict on
  the monolithic encoding \alert{diverge}. Partiality deletes letters for both
  players, and the two encodings do not delete the same ones.

  \vspace{2mm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item This is an \textbf{open theory question}, not a bug in either tool.
    \item It is why the benchmark race is restricted to the tier where the
          monolithic encoding is known legitimate.
    \item The reduction is a \textbf{conjecture}; nothing here proves it.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{2mm}
  {\scriptsize Witness and full discussion:
  \texttt{docs/runs/2026-08-09-acceptance-mark-edgeless.md}, and
  \texttt{tools-and-capabilities.md} §4f.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Appendix --- why the build type mattered}

  The same sweep, Debug vs Release, speedup of \texttt{OtfMtdfaProduct} at
  $n=8$:

  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \toprule
    family & Debug & \alert{Release} \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{cons-prunes}        & 3.09 & \textbf{4.37} \\
    \texttt{cons-inert}         & 0.39 & \textbf{0.91} \\
    \texttt{mirror-small}       & 0.42 & \textbf{0.92} \\
    \texttt{mirror-degenerate}  & 0.40 & \textbf{0.95} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \vspace{2mm}
  Debug materially understated the method: it read as ``wins 3$\times$ here,
  costs you 2.5$\times$ everywhere else''. The methods do not pay equally for
  assertions, so cross-method ratios are not build-portable.
